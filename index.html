What this measures, in one paragraph

Not every ball matters equally. A six when 60 are needed off 24 barely changes who wins; the same six when 10 are needed off 12 almost settles it. This model scores every delivery by how much it could swing the result, and by how much the match itself mattered in the tournament. A player's score is what they did on the balls that mattered, compared with what a typical player does in exactly that situation, against that quality of opposition.

How to read the numbers

Pressure score — a standardised score. 0 is an average eligible player; 1.0 is roughly one standard deviation better. It is not runs or wickets.

Pressure balls — deliveries in the top quarter of match-importance. A player needs at least 120 of them to be eligible.

Chance of being a real top-10 — we re-ran the whole ranking on 1000 resamples of the matches. This is the share of those runs in which the player made the top 10 and cleared the minimum-balls bar. Below about 50%, treat the placing as unproven.

Construction

pressure(ball) = leverage(game state) × [(1−λ) + λ·stakes(match)] × momentum, normalised to mean 1. Leverage is the expected absolute win-probability swing of one delivery under the league-average outcome mix. Stakes are P(qualify | win) − P(qualify | lose), by exact convolution over the points table. Ball value = (expected runs − conceded) + w·(wicket − E[wicket]), where the expectation models see only the opponent's quality, never the rated player's. Composite weights are each component's measured split-half reliability. Every constant is estimated: λ —, wicket run-value — runs, shrinkage k by empirical Bayes.

Weights derived on ≤2024, reliability measured on unseen 2025; selection tested on held-out 2026 with 10,000-shuffle permutation tests, Benjamini–Hochberg corrected.

Known limitations

First-innings win probability is weak (powerplay AUC 0.563 on holdout), and the innings-1 powerplay still supplies about 23% of high-pressure balls against a 15.8% base rate, so that phase is over-weighted relative to how well it is measured.

Per-ball expectation R² is 0.014–0.019: a single T20 delivery is close to irreducible, which caps every per-ball value metric's reliability.

The batter composite tilts toward middle-order finishers (median batting position 5 vs 3 for the rest of the field; 37.6% vs 18.5% of pressure balls at the death), so it may partly measure role rather than temperament.

Season lists are descriptive. No player managed more than two top-10 finishes in five seasons.
